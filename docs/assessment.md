# Assessment write-up — draft for personal review

Delivery problems can arrive as separate driver texts and customer calls, leaving teammates to reconstruct the same context across tools. I chose a shared delivery incident: one report exposes the wider potential impact, and subsequent reports add context to the same response. This uses known information for earlier intervention; it does not predict failures. I assumed Support handles communication and Operations coordinates recovery, while recognizing that responsibilities may overlap.

I built a React/TypeScript prototype with a report inbox and incident workspace. The fictional scenario covers a breakdown with six unfinished deliveries, a linked customer call, proposed and confirmed recovery, simulated customer updates, owned actions with follow-up times, a shared timeline, and resolution with an outcome. Essential work must be completed or explicitly cancelled with a reason. Local persistence and reset make the workflow repeatable; local state provides no real team synchronization. I left out real SMS/calls, route optimization, predictive AI, automated decisions, production identity, and unrelated analytics.

The next step I would propose is connecting this dashboard to the delivery app, so Support and Operations can send live updates to affected customers directly from the incident workspace. For example, after confirming a replacement driver, Support could notify the relevant customers in the app and keep the update attached to the incident. This follows the assessment’s emphasis on a more preventive, less reactive approach: use a known incident to notify other affected customers before they need to contact Support. The business goal is to give customers timely, consistent information and reduce avoidable status inquiries. This would anticipate communication needs, rather than predict or prevent the breakdown itself. I would first validate the app’s existing communication capabilities and test this flow with operators and customers. I would evaluate time to the first customer update, repeat status inquiries, and whether updates reach the intended customers. This is a product proposal, not an implemented integration or a measured improvement.

I used AI to help implement the prototype, create mock data, write tests and documentation, and review the code. I set the product direction, reviewed the browser walkthrough, and questioned friction such as repeated cancellation reasons. I remain responsible for checking the final behavior and understanding the technical decisions.
